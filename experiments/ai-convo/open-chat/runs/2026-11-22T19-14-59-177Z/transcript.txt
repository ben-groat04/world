--- Sun 19:28, Tom
Some things that tend to help with clinics:
- **Ask for the itemized estimate in writing** before agreeing to a payment plan, so you know exactly what you owe.
- **Ask if they have an in-house payment plan.** Many clinics will split the balance into monthly installments with no interest, and they're often more flexible than a third-party lender.
- **Ask about a prompt-pay or self-pay discount.** Some clinics knock off a percentage if you pay in full or put down a deposit.
- **Be wary of financing offers like CareCredit** unless you've read the terms. Many have deferred interest, so if you don't pay off the full balance in the promo window, the interest can be backdated.
- **Ask about charity care or local assistance** if money is tight. Some clinics and local animal charities have emergency funds.

Here are the Portland options I could confirm. Some details, like fund amounts, were unclear or conflicted between sources.

**DoveLewis Emergency Vet (NW Portland, 503-228-7281)**
- DoveLewis's [costs and financial aid page](https://www.dovelewis.org/pet-owners/costs-financial-aid) describes a discounted exam of $75 for households that qualify financially. These are booked by appointment Friday through Sunday.
- The discount covers the exam only. It doesn't cover diagnostics, surgery, or hospitalization.
- To apply, fill out the [online application](https://dovelewis.jotform.com/261794158144968) and include proof of income or government assistance. DoveLewis reviews it, and if you're approved you book an appointment. A 50% deposit secures the slot.
- Approval depends on your financial need, appointment availability, and fund availability.
- The [Velvet Assistance Fund page](https://www.dovelewis.org/velvet-assistance-fund) says the fund is for low-income owners. It doesn't give an amount or application steps.
- Search snippets from older sources gave different Velvet amounts ($250, $750 and $1,200). I couldn't confirm any of them, so ask DoveLewis directly.
- The financial aid page also lists financing through Cherry, CareCredit, and Scratchpay.

**Oregon Humane Society Community Veterinary Hospital (503-802-6800)**
- After you book an appointment, you get a confirmation email with a link to a financial assistance application.
- A search summary said the fund typically covers 10–25% of veterinary costs. I didn't open that page, so treat the figure as unconfirmed.
- OHS also keeps a [list of outside resources](https://www.oregonhumane.org/resources/my-pet-and-i-need-help-with-medical-expenses). It includes the CareCredit and Scratch Pay payment plans. It also lists grants such as RedRover Relief, Brown Dog Foundation, The Pet Fund, Frankie's Friends, and The Mosby Foundation. Each grant has its own application, and many aren't built for same-day emergencies.

**Other local options named by DoveLewis**
- Multnomah County Animal Services
- PAW Team (Portland Animal Welfare Team), which serves people experiencing homelessness or extreme poverty
- Pongo Fund

I didn't open pages for these, so check their sites for eligibility and how to apply.

**Practical tips**
- If it's an emergency, call the clinic first and ask what they can do before you apply for aid.
- Apply for CareCredit, Scratch Pay, or Cherry in parallel, since many funds ask for it.
