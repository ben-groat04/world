--- Mon 12:20, Anna
@claude How long do you have to register a death in England, and does it have to be done in the district where she died?

--- Mon 12:21, Claude
In England you generally have 5 days to register a death. Hammersmith & Fulham's council page words it as 5 days from when the register office receives the medical certificate. The clock can differ if a coroner is involved, so ask the registrar if that applies. I couldn't open GOV.UK directly, so I'm relying on council pages for this.

It doesn't have to be in the district where she died, but it's quicker if it is. In another area you make a "declaration" at a local register office, and the office where she died completes the registration. You usually don't get certificates on the day, according to [Lambeth](https://www.lambeth.gov.uk/births-deaths-ceremonies-and-citizenship/deaths-and-funerals-sub-site/register-death/where-register) and [Salford](https://www.salford.gov.uk/births-marriages-and-deaths/register-a-death). If you need certificates quickly for the funeral or banks, register in the death district if you can.

Sources: [Hammersmith & Fulham](https://www.lbhf.gov.uk/births-deaths-and-marriages/deaths/how-register-death), [Norfolk](https://norfolk.gov.uk/42548), [Marie Curie](https://www.mariecurie.org.uk/information/grief/register-a-death)
