--- Mon 12:21, Claude
Short answer: the usual deadline in England is 5 days from the death. That's from my general knowledge, because I couldn't open GOV.UK and the pages I did find didn't state it. It can be extended in some cases, for example if the coroner is involved. Check this with the registrar.

Who can register: usually a relative (by blood or marriage). If none is available, the other options are someone who was present at the death, a personal representative, or the person arranging the funeral. Hospital administrators and the occupier of the building where the person died can also do it. Councils' wording varies slightly.

Post: I found nothing saying you can do it by post. Councils generally ask you to book an appointment at the register office, usually the one for the area where the death happened. Ask the office whether they can do any part of it remotely.

Your hospital letter may not be the medical certificate of cause of death, which the registrar needs. Ask the hospital's bereavement office or the registrar whether it has already been sent to the register office. You may not need to hold it yourself.

Sources: council register office pages (Lambeth, Haringey, Newcastle, Bolton, Kingston) and Marie Curie.
