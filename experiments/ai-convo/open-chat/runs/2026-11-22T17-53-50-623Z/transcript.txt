**Short answer: I can't confirm that the Riemann hypothesis has been proved. There is a fresh claim, but it hasn't been accepted.**

**The claim**
- Levent Alpöge, who is at Anthropic, says he proved the Riemann hypothesis with Claude. It was posted on 20 November 2026 as a short PDF note at alpo.ge/remark.pdf. It points to a Lean formalization archive of 14 parts, reported as about 175,000 files. There is no conventional paper or journal submission. ([State of Proof](https://stateofproof.org/paper-watch/paper-2026-11-20-a-claimed-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis))

**Checking so far**
- State of Proof lists its examination as "not started" and records no expert reactions. It also says the match between the Lean statements and the standard hypothesis still needs checking. ([State of Proof](https://stateofproof.org/paper-watch/paper-2026-11-20-a-claimed-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis))
- Shattered.io says the Lean release is "released, not independently validated". It reports no outside mathematician has verified it. ([Shattered](https://shattered.io/claude-riemann-hypothesis-claim-alpoge-lean-proof-zero-outside-checks/))
- **Conflicting report:** BigGo quotes a joint statement dated 21 November from the Lean and Mathlib community, endorsed by Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative. It says they have "full confidence" the proof is correct. ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/c132374d-8b9c-4693-91fc-616afae88090)) I couldn't find this statement in any primary source or second outlet. The State of Proof page doesn't mention it either. Treat it as unverified.
- I didn't open the PDF or the Lean archive myself. My account of them comes from secondary sources.
- The Clay Mathematics Institute prize requires peer-reviewed publication and a waiting period. I found no sign it has recognized any proof. I didn't find a current Clay page that confirms the problem is still listed as open.

**Confidence**
- **High:** The claim exists, who made it, and when it was posted.
- **High:** It hasn't gone through peer review or prize recognition. That's only a few days after posting, so it couldn't have.
- **Low to moderate:** Whether the proof is correct, and whether the Lean community has endorsed it. A Lean kernel check would show the formal statement is proved. It wouldn't show that the statement is the Riemann hypothesis, and that mismatch is the main risk.

To follow it, check the Lean Zulip, Mathlib or Lean FRO's own announcements, and Clay's website.
