--- Mon 08:01, Claude → Tom (private)
I confirmed the Leeds council process and the Renters' Rights Act timing. I couldn't open the legislation.gov.uk or gov.uk pages, so the statute details below come from my own knowledge. Check them against the links.

**1. Landlord's duties**
- **Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 s.11** ([text](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1985/70/section/11)) applies to most tenancies under 7 years. It requires the landlord to keep the structure and exterior in repair, and also the installations for water, gas, electricity, sanitation, heating and hot water. A bathroom ceiling is part of the structure. A leak from a roof or from pipes above it is normally the landlord's responsibility. The duty only starts once the landlord knows about the problem, so report it in writing.
- **Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) Act 2018** (it inserts ss.9A–9C into the 1985 Act) requires the home to be fit to live in at the start of the tenancy and throughout it. A ceiling that is collapsing, or that has a persistent leak and mould, can breach it. A tenant can sue in the county court, and the court can order repairs and award compensation.
- **Housing Act 2004, Housing Health and Safety Rating System (HHSRS)** covers hazards such as damp and mould, falls, and electrical hazards (for example, a leak near light fittings). The council scores hazards as Category 1 (serious) or Category 2.

**2. Deadlines**
- Section 11 has no fixed number of days. The landlord must act within a "reasonable time", which depends on the urgency. A ceiling that is leaking or about to fall is urgent.
- Leeds City Council tells tenants to report in writing and allow about 14 days for a response. For urgent problems it suggests contacting the council once the landlord has failed to act ([Leeds: help for private tenants](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/housing/help-for-private-tenants)).
- **Awaab's Law** (fixed timescales for damp and mould) applies to social landlords. For private landlords, my search suggests the Renters' Rights Act only gives government the power to extend it. The same search suggests a consultation on doing so runs to 18 December 2026. Those sources were commercial guides rather than gov.uk, so treat this as unconfirmed. The same sources say the Decent Homes Standard for private rentals is not due until 2035. The main Renters' Rights Act tenancy reforms began on 1 May 2026.

**3. Leeds City Council routes**
- Contact the Private Rented Housing team on 0113 378 4699 or at prs.housing@leeds.gov.uk. The council says it will usually speak to you first, may ask for photos and correspondence, and may arrange an inspection ([source](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/housing/help-for-private-tenants)).
- If an inspection finds serious hazards, the council can take enforcement action. Under the Housing Act 2004 (from my own

knowledge), it can serve an improvement notice, a prohibition order or an emergency remedial action notice. It can also carry out the works itself and charge the landlord, and it can issue civil penalties of up to £30,000 per offence. Failing to comply with an improvement notice is an offence. I couldn't open the Act itself, so check the details on [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/34/contents).

Leeds has used these powers. In December 2025 it prosecuted a Beeston landlord over damp, mould and heating. The landlord had failed to comply with an improvement notice and had received civil penalties totalling £37,000 since 2021 ([Leeds news](https://news.leeds.gov.uk/news/landlord-pays-price-for-mismanagement-of-leeds-rental-property)).

**4. Other routes if the landlord stalls**
- **Court action:** A tenant can claim for disrepair under s.11 or the 2018 Act in the county court. Shelter's guidance covers this. I didn't open it, so I can't confirm its details.
- **Ombudsman:** If the council fails to act on your report, you can complain to the [Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman](https://www.lgo.org.uk). It has closed several Leeds private-housing complaints without investigation where it found no fault, for example [25 013 847](https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/housing/private-housing/25-013-847). Complete the council's own complaints process first.
- **Caution on claims firms:** Leeds warns about disrepair claims firms ([Leeds council page](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/campaign/housing-disrepair-claims)). I didn't open that page, so I don't know the details.

**Practical steps**
1. Report the problem in writing, with dated photos, and keep a copy.
2. If nothing happens after about 14 days, or sooner if the ceiling is unsafe, contact the council team above.
3. Don't withhold rent. That risks arrears and eviction.

**Not confirmed:** I haven't verified the exact penalty amounts or the full text of the statutes. I also haven't confirmed the current status of the Awaab's Law extension to private tenants. If you tell me your tenancy type and start date, I can check how these rules apply to you.
