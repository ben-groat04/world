--- Mon 07:25, Claude
The deadline is **5 days**, and it doesn't run from the date of death. GOV.UK says it runs from when the medical examiner's office contacts you to confirm you can register. Weekends and bank holidays count.

- **Exact wording:** I opened GOV.UK's "Register a death" guidance for England and Wales. It says: "You must register the death within 5 days (8 days in Scotland) of being contacted."
- **Medical examiner step:** Since deaths that don't go to a coroner are now reviewed by an NHS medical examiner, the clock starts at that confirmation. It doesn't start when the GP writes the certificate.
- **Older rule:** The Births and Deaths Registration Act 1953 sets the same 5 days. Councils such as Staffordshire describe it as running from the register office receiving the medical certificate. I couldn't open that page (it returned an error), so I'm relying on the search summary for it. Search results also gave me the wording from the GOV.UK pages above. I couldn't confirm the Act's text directly.
- **In practice:** The medical examiner's office or the GP surgery should tell you when the certificate has gone to the register office. Book your appointment after that. If you're near the 5 days, tell the register office, which can advise on any delay.

Sources:
- [GOV.UK: Register a death (England and Wales, at home/hospital)](https://www.gov.uk/register-a-death/y/england_wales/at_home_hospital/no)
- [Staffordshire County Council: When can I register a death?](https://www.staffordshire.gov.uk/births-ceremonies-and-deaths/death-and-bereavement/register-death/when-can-i-register-death) (via search summary only)
- [Births and Deaths Registration Act 1953, Part II](https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Eliz2/1-2/20/part/II/2020-03-26/data.html) (not opened)
