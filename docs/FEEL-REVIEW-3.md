# Larkfield feel review 3 (fresh-eyes, skeptical Brit)

Method: played the stable built copy (not the live repo) in Chromium, mostly iPhone 13 emulation plus desktop 1280x800. I did not read the design docs, ISSUES.md or code comments first. Roughly 50 minutes of interaction: Lifetime / x3 / x8 / Max across about 20 game years, 15+ residents' full profiles read (several re-read years later), the feed, Storylines, Forum and Posts; played Adam Watson (job application, market stall, Go/Phone/Goals) and Khalid Hamdi (Talk); god mode (rent doubled, library cut). Screenshots are in `docs/screenshots/review3-*.png`. `tools/report.js --seed 1 --years 10` timed out in my session (killed at 200s), so I have no headline-stat check from it. Real-world figures below are from my own knowledge, not web-verified, and are marked as such.

## Verdict

- A handsome, genuinely charming and well-engineered toy: the map, the voice of the local news ("a cake gone in eleven minutes", "mustn't grumble") and the Play/God depth are well above the usual standard.
- But it breaks under a close read: repeated verbatim punchlines, a lot of internally impossible life-story data (dates, ages, tenure, jobs vs. arrival), and a few UK-specific implausibilities (wages, council tenancies, unemployment, payday loans).
- Fun is strongest in the first ten minutes and then flattens into formulaic "split up then rent arrears" arcs; consequences of god-mode moves are visible but muted.

## Top 10 immersion breakers

1. **Canned punchlines repeat word for word across different people.** In one run of ~60 feed items: "It was always the parking," said a witness (Michelle/Nicola, Natalie/Jack, later Bilal/Laura, Jessica/Lee, Sofia/Shreya, each "sharing a pot of tea at the cafe"); "I had a spreadsheet of reasons" (Troy, Saqib, Dipak); "They used a lot of nouns" (Jacob, Zoe, Amelia); "enough to stop looking at the jobs page" (Joshua, Sophie, Zoe); "The cake, a three-tier Victoria sponge, was reportedly gone in eleven minutes" (every wedding); "Existing residents have offered tips on parking, bin days and which chippy to avoid" (every arrival). Funny once, a tic by the tenth. Also the forum sign-off "Cheers, everyone, and mind the puddles." is stapled onto unrelated posts (a drilling complaint, a freebie). Same pattern in thoughts: "Dreaming about the bus not arriving. It did not come in the dream either." appeared on three different people in a single directory pass.
2. **Life stories contradict themselves on dates.** Ritu Kapoor (70): "Moved to 23 Cutting Road" 2011, but "Moved to the UK from UAE" 2015, and "Moved in together" appears both in 2016 and again in 2027. Khalid Hamdi: "Started work as an IT technician" Aug 2022 and "Moved to 3 Cutting Road" Sep 2023, but "Moved to the UK from Morocco" Dec 2023. Chioma Balogun: "Started work as a private-hire driver" 2022, "Moved to the UK from Nigeria" Jan 2024. Aurélie Atangana (Cameroon): worked as an "airport worker" from 2021, arrived 2025. Mahad Abdullahi, aged 11: "Moved to 55 Cutting Road for work". Mohamed Ali, aged 1: "Moved to Flat 2, Wharf House ... to buy". Backstory is stitched together, not simulated, and it shows the instant you open a profile.
3. **Tenure and ages are stale or inconsistent.** Khalid shows "2 years here" in the header, "3 years" in the story, and the history says 2023 (nearly 5 years, in 2028). Amanuel Negash (37 in 2028, 40 in Jan 2031) still says "9 years 2 months here" and "has been there 9 years" both times. Justin Jackson: "8 years here" vs "9 years". Children's birthdays: Amanuel's twins Aster (16 Jun 2022) and Rediet (26 Mar 2022) are both 6 but were born 12 weeks apart.
4. **Impossible people-in-places.** Fatema Begum, 80, stroke survivor with "mobility impairment", is on the roster of "Sunday five-a-side team" and "Clashed at a meeting at Holly Road Recreation Ground". She "Retired from work" on Christmas Day 2020. Adam Watson's 0-year-old daughter Maria, "you have not met", is in his thought "Sorting dinner while Maria does homework". A 3-year-old (Alfie Smith) "is at school". I could Talk to Khalid's housemate while Khalid's own state was "Asleep at home" at 02:39, and Khalid walked to a pub at 01:00.
5. **GCSEs before GCSEs existed.** Salma (73): "Finished GCSEs" 1970. Fatema (80): GCSEs in 1963. Ritu (born 1958): "Finished GCSEs" 1974. Every adult gets a GCSE milestone, which a Brit under 55 would find fine and anyone older will find daft (O-levels / CSEs, and GCSEs began in 1988).
6. **Job and pay realism.** Offered jobs list "Cleaner / housekeeper £24-27k a year, 20 h/week" (that is about £23/hr; real cleaning pay is nearer the National Living Wage, from my knowledge) and "Hospitality / leisure worker £24-29k, 30 h/week". "Private-hire driver, 46 h/wk, £20,307/yr" is under minimum wage per hour but plausible as net gig earnings. Amanuel Negash goes from primary school teacher to "Lost his job when the firm closed" (a school is not a firm) to "Started as a nurse, on a friend's word" three months later (no nursing degree route), and is on a zero-hours contract at 39 h/wk. Chioma leaves rideshare driving and is "Started as a baker" ten days later. Career switches have no training or lag.
7. **Housing and benefits.** Chioma Balogun, a single childless 43-year-old with debt, goes from private rent to "Moved in from nearby on a council tenancy" in a few months. Social housing waiting lists for single adults with no priority are measured in years (from my knowledge). Eviction notices are described as "served notice to leave a rented home after a few years" with the boilerplate "A tenants' group says three other households on the same street have received similar letters since September" appearing in a December article and again in a November one: the "since September" is hard-coded. Rented-flat "needs about £894 in hand" deposits are 60% of a month's rent regardless; UK deposit cap is five weeks (about 1.15 months), so the number reads off. "Payday loans in the post" / "payday lenders" is a 2010s image; the 2014 cap and the collapse of the big lenders mean most modern stories would be BNPL, catalogue credit, overdrafts, doorstep lenders or loan sharks.
8. **Tone-deaf micro-posts and seasonal slips.** On 13 Sep 2028 (mild autumn), posts say "Dark at 6pm already", "Scarf weather", "a runner in shorts in 3 degrees" and "someone dragging a Christmas tree in March". "Heard the lunchtime bells from the church around 12.49." (bells strike on the hour). "A kerb dug up for 5 weeks ... Been there since 5 days." "Saw a very sad Christmas tree still out in 4." (garbled). "Beans on toast from Lahore Karahi." Posts use the same skeleton ("Seen on X: a ... Heard: ... Smelled: ... Verdict: fine"), and "Holly Road Recreation Ground" appears in about one in five posts in full, where a local would say "the rec".
9. **Text glue bugs.** "had wordssomething said online", "had wordsthe salon" (missing separator); "does the The Larkfield Arms"; "1 replies" in the forum; "a long-running disagreement" announced as a "Truce at last" for people who had barely met; "an an admin temp", "an a call-centre adviser"; "Born in the south coast." next to "born on the south coast"; "He started borrowing ... (2 times, and in the last few months he fell out ..." (unclosed bracket); "stomach bug for 0 days before an ambulance was called"; "The Larkfield Kutz" as a club meeting venue (a mash-up of the pub and the barber). Job titles keep their taxonomy slashes in the prose: "Krzysztof walks out of a freelance designer/writer/photographer job", "food factory/agency packer", "Ryan finished a business ... pulled off a business again" (the storyline engine's wording for a venture, unreadable).
10. **Demographic and money numbers that do not fit UK urban life.** Town says unemployment 0.7% ("271 employed · 2 looking") yet the digest reports 44-77 "jobs lost" in 15-26 months; the UK rate is around 4-5%, and inner-city areas run higher (from my knowledge). Deaths: 3-4 over 15-26 months for 560 people with 52 "older locals" (a UK crude rate would give about 5 a year). Loneliness 3.2% looks low. 20-39 break-ups against 4-15 weddings. Household "monthly income £11,136" for a 6-sharer house is shown as the person's money panel, and "Borrowing at high cost to cover essentials" appears next to "£8,893 saved, £0 debt, spare £5,860". "Rent burden 30% / Money strained / spare £773" also contradict each other. Aurélie Atangana shows "-£669 saved · £669 debt" (double counted).

Also noticed, lower confidence: Aurélie (260) and Khalid (3) show a "Died" tag in the inspector text while their activity text says they are asleep and Khalid is still listed at his Clarendon Road address in 2030-31 after "Moved out to the city" in May 2029. It may be a hidden/stale badge. I could not confirm.

## Top 5 fun gaps

1. **Storylines are formulaic.** Of the ten or so arcs I read, most are "split up, then behind on the rent" or "New to the street: welcomed by X, Y and Z". After a few minutes you know the shape of every story, and there is no payoff that makes you care (no sense of who is lonely, who is rising, who is about to fall apart).
2. **No emotional hooks on the feed.** Headlines carry the same register for a dementia diagnosis, a wedding and a bin round. Everyone is "being careful about who sits where". There is little of "this person again" continuity in the Watch feed (you see Hana again with debts, but never "Hana finally cleared it").
3. **Play mode early hours are shallow.** The "Things to try" panel arrives 4/5 complete (meet neighbours, join a group, make a friend, set a goal are already Done), so the tutorial is meaningless. Applying for a job works smoothly but the reply is generic ("they went with someone else"). A market stall's results are hidden behind "monthly takings settle into Me", with no visible stall, customers or day-to-day feedback. Talk lines are good ("Yes, mustn't grumble," and a polite nod) but a few cooldown messages (" You have only just spoken to Patrick") make the conversation feel like a cooldown timer.
4. **God-mode consequences are muted.** I doubled every private rent (Khalid's room £1,965 to £3,930, Gemma's flat to £2,820). Over the next three months the news showed arrears 11 to 16, debt spirals 3 to 8, notices 1 to 7, moves out 7 to 13 (feed window only, a rough count). It is real and legible in the Consequences panel, but a 100% rent rise would break a real street; five years on, the panel still says "Nothing notable yet" while listing 747 events. Library cut: 168 events logged, but no visible campaign headline that I found.
5. **Pacing.** At x3 and above, headlines fly past and the "While you were fast-forwarding" digest is the only memory. There is no way to say "tell me about this one person", and following people across years means opening their profile repeatedly and finding that the history list rolls off (Khalid's 18 entries became 9; Chioma's 35 became 23).

## Top 5 UX issues

1. **On a phone, the sheet and the map fight.** The Watch/People/Town/God sheet covers most of the map, and the "While you were fast-forwarding" digest card floats over the map and the controls (`review3-m-god.png`). Selecting a person from the map does not obviously switch tabs; I had to be on People first.
2. **Speed menu is two taps away, and Lifetime moves far faster than "lifetime" suggests.** Lifetime is about a game-year in 45 seconds on a phone (1.3 game-years per minute on desktop). "Scene" is the only speed that lets you read. The desktop has the pill row; on mobile it is hidden behind a drop-down labelled with the current tier name.
3. **Play-mode menu is dense and jargon-heavy.** "Look into opportunity automatic / happens on its own at home / Volunteer for a hand automatic" is repeated three times; "Life admin", "Allow a start-up loan", "Ways forward for you" are unclear. A reader has to scroll a long page to find jobs, money and housing in one sheet, with the mortgage section tapering into rented flats "tap to move" with no confirmation I could see.
4. **Truncated chips and cramped lists.** The People filter chips cut off at the edge ("Older locals", "families", "Lone parents", "Stud..."): the first two read as "Older locals families". On a phone the inspector shows long run-together text blocks (the Life story block lists 20-35 items in a single scroll with the date glued to the previous line in text copies).
5. **Map clutter at mid zoom.** "To let" labels sit on top of the gold person markers (`review3-m-people-dir.png`); shop labels with white outline overlap in the High Street; the "TIME-LAPSE Autumn 2026" caption blocks a quarter of a phone map; the "Paused" pill sits over the map corner on desktop.

## What works well

- **Voice and local texture.** Victorian street names (Balaclava, Kitchener, Gladstone, Cobden), "Greggs", "Londis", "Mrs Dhillon's Tea Rooms", "Pak-Afro Foods", "Fade Factory" and "Wing Zone" feel true to a UK inner city. "Caff", "Off-licence" and "Chicken shop" are on the business menu; no Americanisms in the UI chrome that I noticed.
- **Posts with specificity are delightful.** "My granddaughter showed me how to video call. I have been waving at the ceiling." "The trainer used the word 'journey' 8 times. I counted." "Bin day is Wednesday, as it says on the calendar and the lid." "I have a list of 8 things" (ward surgery). The WhatsApp snippets ("Who is the man who turned up in jeans?") are on the money.
- **Small human touches.** "Mustn't grumble" as a talk response; chat threads that sound like families; Eritrean-style patronymic surnames for the Negash children (Hanna Amanuel) are a nice, accurate detail.
- **Systems depth is visible and legible.** Profile shows household, rent, landlord, condition %, commute mode and minutes, goals, groups and 20-50 relationships with sources (school gate, faith, pub). Play mode offers a rich Talk menu (Easy / Closer / Tense), Phone, Go with a map of venues, Life admin with jobs, loans, stall, units to let, and "expect about 16 days" for a job reply. The market stall flow clearly states cost, risk and the "six loss-making months and it closes" rule.
- **God catalogue is broad and honest.** Presets with plain "what would happen" blurbs, an Undo for rent shock ("Roll rents back by 50%"), a per-intervention Consequences tracker with counts, and a "Most changes cannot be undone" warning.
- **Desktop layout is polished** (`review3-d-storylines.png`, `review3-d-inspect.png`): a clear speed pill row, a clean map with street labels, a useful town dashboard (rent index, wellbeing, age pyramid, who lives here).

## Framing against PRINCIPLES.md

This review mostly hits principle 4 (read the traces yourself and fix what reads wrong) and principle 7 (cite evidence for UK-specific mechanisms: council-tenancy allocation, deposit caps, unemployment, death rates, minimum wage). The dated backstory problems (item 2, 3, 5) look like synthesised day-one history rather than emergent state, so they are fixable at generation time without touching dynamics. The repeated lines (item 1) are a content-variety problem: a pool of 5-10 variants per template, keyed on the people's relationship and the season, would remove most of the "tic" effect.
