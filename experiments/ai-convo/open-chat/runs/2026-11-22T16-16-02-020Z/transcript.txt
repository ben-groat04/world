Here's my opening take: a good explanation lets the listener *predict or reconstruct* something they couldn't before. It's not just that it sounds satisfying. Plenty of bad explanations feel complete but can't be used to anticipate anything. A good one also fits the listener's starting point, so it connects to what they already know, and it makes clear what would count as it being wrong.

A test I like: after hearing it, can you handle a slightly different case from the one you were given? If so, you probably got an explanation rather than just a story.

--- round 1, D
Hi A, B, and C, I'm D. I like C's test of handling a slightly different case, and I'd add two things.

**1. A good explanation depends on the question being asked.** "Why did the bridge collapse?" can be answered with "resonance," "a design flaw," or "budget cuts," and each is good relative to a different contrast: why *this* bridge and not another, or why *then* and not earlier. Many bad explanations answer a question nobody asked. So part of explaining well is working out the contrast the listener has in mind.

**2. Good explanations are hard to vary.** This is David Deutsch's idea. If you can change the details of an explanation and it still accounts for everything, the details weren't doing any work. "The seasons happen because Persephone is sad" can be tweaked to fit any outcome. The tilted-axis account can't be, because the tilt fixes which hemisphere is warm when, so it forbids many things. This connects to C's point about what would count as being wrong.

On accuracy versus simplicity, I think the tradeoff is real but gets misread. The aim isn't the simplest account. It's the simplest one that keeps the causal structure the listener will need to reason with. A good analogy preserves that structure (electric current as water flow works for pressure and resistance, but fails for things like inductance), and a good explainer says where the analogy breaks.

A question for you both: do you think an explanation can be good for one person and bad for another, or is "good" mostly about the explanation itself?
