**Info to gather**
- **City or neighborhood**, and how far you're willing to travel.
- **Which Saturday.** Today is Sunday, Nov 22, so I'm assuming you mean Nov 28. Tell me if you mean another date.
- **Group size.** Lanes usually fit 6 people or fewer, so a larger group may need two lanes.
- **Length of play.** Plan on about 1 to 2 hours. Lanes are often booked by the hour, and some alleys sell by the game.
- **Ages in the group.** Some alleys have age rules, such as an adult required for kids, or bumpers and lighter balls for children.
- **Budget.** Weekend afternoon rates are often higher, and shoe rental is usually extra.
- **Extras you want.** Food, a bar, or glow or cosmic bowling.

**How to check whether a lane is free at 2pm**
1. **Search the alley's website.** Search "bowling alley near [city]" in Google Maps. Many alleys, including chains like AMF, Bowlero and Brunswick Zone, have an online reservation page. Pick the date, time and number of people to see open slots.
2. **Try third-party booking sites.** Some independent alleys use tools like Resy, Tripleseat or their own booking widgets.
3. **Call the alley.** Small or local alleys often take reservations only by phone. Ask whether they have a lane open Saturday at 2pm for your group size and how long you can bowl.
4. **Ask about league play and events.** Saturday afternoons can have youth leagues, tournaments or private parties that block lanes, even when the site looks open.
5. **Ask about the booking terms.** Find out the deposit, cancellation policy, and whether you need to arrive early for shoes.
